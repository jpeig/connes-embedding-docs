Connes' embedding problem has a negative answer, and the operator-algebraic derivation of it is verified here in full by the Lean 4 kernel against Mathlib — from a synchronous-game separation down to a tracial state admitting no trace-preserving embedding into any matrix ultrapower. Exactly one input comes from outside operator algebra, the MIP*=RE separation, and it is cited rather than assumed away: #print axioms names it, and names nothing else beyond propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. No declaration in the development carries a sorry, so no theorem reports sorryAx.

Proof status, in four lines.

  • Main theorem (§3): proved from Lean's three standard Mathlib axioms and the single domain-specific premise; no sorryAx.
  • That premise: a consequence of published theorems (JNVWY and the equivalences behind it), cited rather than reproved. Its complete formal statement is printed in §3.
  • Spectral truncations (§7): independent of CEP, and closed — the finite-stage Rieffel compact-quantum-metric-space structure, using no domain-specific premise. Convergence to the limit is not claimed.
  • Complexity-free routes (§4–§6): separate from the main theorem, and conditional — every open input is an explicit hypothesis of the statement that uses it. No declaration in this development carries a sorry.

1. What this is for

The Connes Embedding Problem has had a negative answer since Ji–Natarajan–Vidick–Wright–Yuen proved MIP*=RE in 2020. The decisive argument is a construction about interactive proof systems, probabilistically checkable proofs and recursion theory. Its operator-algebraic consequence reaches the subject through a long chain of equivalences — the Kirchberg and Tsirelson correspondences, synchronous game algebras, amenable traces, u.c.p. lifting — and that chain has not been written out in a form where each step can be checked independently of the complexity-theoretic construction.

This development addresses that in three specific ways.

The operator-algebraic implication is checkable in full. Every step from the premise to the non-embeddable tracial state — the dilation and lifting arguments, finite-dimensional liftability, free-product permanence, the u.c.p. extraction and the final composition — is re-checked by the kernel. The check is mechanical: it passes or it does not.

The dependence on complexity theory is one premise, and that premise is a formal object. It is printed in full in §3, so it can be read, criticized as a rendering of what MIP*=RE supplies, and — this is the point — replaced. A proof of that single statement yields a fully verified refutation of CEP with no further work, because everything beneath it is already checked. The distance from "¬CEP on trust" to "¬CEP verified" is exactly this one statement.

Where the difficulty sits is stated precisely. A complexity-free route needs an explicit non-hyperlinear group. §4 formalizes the reduction skeleton with its two open cores as named hypotheses, and §6 records exactly what the known attacks do and do not establish — so the difficulty is localized to statements one can quote. Section 7, by contrast, is closed.

What this does not do. It does not reprove MIP*=RE, and it does not show the complexity-free route succeeds.

What it does do is worth stating without hedging. The premise of §3 is not an open question: it is a consequence of published, accepted results. Everything from that premise down to a non-Connes-embeddable tracial state is verified by the kernel. So ¬CEP is machine-checked here modulo one cited input — the ordinary position of any argument that invokes a prior theorem rather than reproving it. The residual question is not whether the input is true, but whether the statement in §3 renders it faithfully; that entailment is assembled by hand rather than by the kernel, which is why the statement is printed in full rather than paraphrased.

2. Verified, assumed, open — what each word means here

These are three separate axes, and a claim about one says nothing about the others. Two of them are now empty here — nothing is assumed except one premise, and nothing is left unfinished — so the distinction matters mainly for reading what the conditional routes of §4 do and do not say.

Verified means the kernel re-checked every inference. A Lean proof is not accepted because a program judged it plausible; it is re-checked by a small trusted core. If the development compiles, each theorem is a theorem of the ambient logic — given the assumptions listed for it.

Assumed means an axiom: an unproved formal premise. Assumptions are tracked and cannot be hidden. For any theorem T, #print axioms T reports the complete list of unproved statements its proof rests on, transitively; a premise used three layers down still appears. The list is computed by the system, not chosen by the author, so it cannot be shortened by omission.

Such a list always contains propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound — propositional extensionality, choice, and the soundness of quotients. These are assumptions, but they are the ambient logic of essentially every development built on Mathlib, and they are not assumptions about operator algebras. So the meaningful measure is not how few entries appear, but what the domain-specific ones say. Here there is exactly one, and it is the entire mathematical trust surface of the main theorem.

A premise being intended to encode a cited theorem is a separate matter from its being correctly stated. Whether kirchberg_game_separation_ucp_structured faithfully renders what MIP*=RE supplies is a mathematical question about its statement, not something the kernel can answer — which is why it is printed in full rather than described.

Open means a sorry: an unfinished proof, declared as such. These are tracked the same way: anything depending on one reports sorryAx. That makes the reconciliation mechanical rather than a matter of assurance:

Verified Assumed Unfinished (sorryAx)
Main theorem (§3) the whole implication one domain-specific premise none
Complexity-free routes (§4) the implications themselves, at the ambient logic nothing — every open input is a hypothesis in the signature none
Spectral truncations (§7) the finite-stage CQMS structure nothing beyond the ambient logic none

A citation is not an open problem, and the two must not be read alike. The main theorem of §3 has no hypotheses: it concludes outright that a non-embeddable tracial state exists. What it depends on is an axiom — visible in #print axioms, not in the statement — and that axiom records a published theorem. Cite-and-use is what every mathematical argument does with prior work; the only unusual thing here is that the citation is legible, because the kernel forces it into a printed list instead of a bibliography entry.

The routes of §4 are a different species. Their assumptions sit in their signatures, and those assumptions are open problems — nobody can supply them, as §6 explains. Calling both situations "conditional" would flatten the distinction that matters: one result stands on the literature, the other on things that do not yet exist.

The third column is empty by construction rather than by luck. Where the complexity-free routes once invoked unproved link lemmas inside their proofs — so that they looked like theorems while depending on sorryAx, and therefore established nothing — every such link is now a named hypothesis of the statement that uses it. The two entry points consequently report

'ZFib.CEP.qce_implies_cep_false' depends on axioms: [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]
'ZFib.CEP.acm_implies_cep_false' depends on axioms: [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]

and they say something true: given these named inputs, a non-Connes-embeddable tracial state exists. Neither can be mistaken for a proof of ¬CEP, because each displays exactly what it assumes. What is open is visible in a signature instead of hidden in a proof.

3. The main theorem and its single premise

The statement, in FaithfulSingleCapstone.lean:

theorem cep_false_operator_algebraic_single_scaffold :
    ZFib.CEP.ConnesNonEmbeddableWitness

ConnesNonEmbeddableWitness asserts the existence of a C*-algebra carrying a tracial state that admits no trace-preserving embedding into a tracial matrix ultrapower. It is not an abbreviation for something weaker: unfolded, it ends in matrix algebras, an ultrafilter, and an injective trace-preserving *-homomorphism, with the ultrapower pinned by an isomorphism to the concrete quotient of matrix sequences. So a weak surrogate cannot pass for a refutation of CEP.

The single domain-specific premise, in full:

axiom kirchberg_game_separation_ucp_structured :
    ∃ (X : Type) (_hX : Fintype X) (m : ℕ) (_hm : Nonempty (Fin m))
      (B : Type) (instCB : CStarAlgebra B) (instPO : PartialOrder B)
      (instSOR : StarOrderedRing B)
      (τ : @TracialState B instCB instPO instSOR)
      (_S : @MeasurementAlgebraStructure X _hX m B instCB instPO instSOR),
      ¬ @IsAmenableTraceUCP B instCB instPO instSOR τ

Read out: there are a finite question set X and outcome count m, and a C*-algebra B carrying a tracial state τ and the structure of the universal measurement algebra on X and m — the n-fold free product of m-outcome PVM algebras, C*(F(n,m)) ≅ ∗ⁿ Cᵐ — such that τ admits no u.c.p.-amenable approximation.

This is one formal premise, but it packages several separately published identifications, and each deserves its own citation rather than a collective label:

What the premise asserts Where it comes from
a finite synchronous game separating the quantum-commuting from the quantum-approximate correlations Ji–Natarajan–Vidick–Wright–Yuen, arXiv:2001.04383: Theorem 12.10 ("An explicit separation") gives val*(𝔊^SEP) ≤ 1/2 and val^co(𝔊^SEP) = 1; Theorem 12.6 is the underlying dichotomy and Corollary 12.9 is MIP*=RE itself. Synchronicity of the game is stated in §1.2 and §1.4
that separation is equivalent to ¬CEP Fritz, arXiv:1008.1168; Junge–Navascués–Palazuelos–Pérez-García–Scholz–Werner, arXiv:1008.1142 — the implication JNVWY invokes for CEP in its §1.3
the same equivalence restricted to synchronous correlations Dykema–Paulsen, arXiv:1503.07207, Thm 3.7
a perfect qc strategy ⟺ a tracial state on C*(F(n,m)) with the right values prior work, cited as [PSS⁺16] in Kim–Paulsen–Schafhauser §3 and stated there in prose — not a numbered result of that paper
a perfect qa strategy ⟺ an amenable trace on C*(F(n,m)) Kim–Paulsen–Schafhauser, arXiv:1707.01016, Thm 3.6(3); the game-level form is their Cor. 3.7
"amenable trace" = approximable by unital completely positive maps into matrix algebras, with defect measured in the trace 2-norm Brown, arXiv:math/0304009, Thm 3.1.6(2): "there exists a sequence of u.c.p. maps φ_n : A → M_{k(n)}(ℂ) such that ‖φ_n(ab) − φ_n(a)φ_n(b)‖₂ → 0 and τ(a) = lim tr_{k(n)} ∘ φ_n(a)"

Two points a referee should know. First, the u.c.p. requirement in the last row is exactly what IsAmenableTraceUCP encodes, so the premise assumes the literature's notion and not a stronger one. An earlier packaging of this development assumed a non-u.c.p. notion of amenable trace, which is strictly stronger than the cited results supply; that axiom and the theorem it fed have been removed rather than documented, so the statement above is the only axiom declaration in the repository — a claim worth checking with grep -rn "^axiom" ZFib, which returns one line. Second, the trace is asserted to live on the universal free product C*(F(n,m)) itself rather than on a game-specific quotient; that is the form Kim–Paulsen–Schafhauser's Thm 3.6 states, so it tracks the literature rather than strengthening it.

Checking that the statement above says what those papers actually provide is the one piece of mathematics a reader must do by hand. Two details worth knowing before doing it. JNVWY state the strict inclusion and the CEP consequence in prose in §1.3 rather than as numbered corollaries — the numbered results are those listed above — so the chain to ¬CEP runs through Fritz and Junge et al. rather than through a single citable line. And the qc half of the dictionary is not part of Kim–Paulsen–Schafhauser's Theorem 3.6: only the qa half is, as item (3); the qc half is earlier work they restate in prose. Everything downstream of it is verified — including free-product permanence of approximate u.c.p. liftability, proved internally as bocaFreeProductPermanence_holds_internal; the classical content it re-derives is due to Boca, Free products of completely positive maps and spectral sets, J. Funct. Anal. 97 (1991), and it is a proof here rather than a citation.

4. The complexity-free routes

Can ¬CEP be reached without MIP*=RE, by exhibiting a non-hyperlinear group? That is open. Formalized here is a reduction skeleton with the open cores as explicit hypotheses:

Rank one (ConditionalChain.lean):

theorem qce_implies_cep_false (p : Nat) [Fact (Nat.Prime p)]
    (hGlue : SerreVertexStabilityWithGluing (serreAmalgamData_p p))
    (hExt : DVCentralExtensionAvailable p)
    (hObstruction : DVObstructionAvailable p)
    (hModel : FaithfulTracialGroupModelAvailable)
    (hQCE : QCE_p p) : ConnesNonEmbeddableWitness

QCE_p is a dimension-uniform Hilbert–Schmidt rigidity statement on the congruence edge of the Bass–Serre amalgam SL₂(ℤ) ∗_{Γ₀(p)} SL₂(ℤ). From it: flexible HS-stability of the amalgam, a Dogon–Vigdorovich central-extension obstruction forcing a non-hyperlinear group, then the group–von-Neumann dictionary.

Higher rank (HighRankChain.lean):

theorem acm_implies_cep_false (L : HighRankLatticeData)
    (hStability : ACMImpliesStability L)
    (hObstruction : HighRankObstruction L)
    (hModel : FaithfulTracialGroupModelAvailable)
    (hACM : ACM L) : ConnesNonEmbeddableWitness

ACM (approximate congruence-monomiality) is the regular-sector half of flexible HS-stability for a higher-rank lattice; the rest uses the Dogon–Vigdorovich 2025 higher-rank (T;FD) mechanism and the same shared tail.

The status of these two is precise, and it is stronger than it was. Both are complete conditional theorems: every open input appears in the signature, nothing is deferred to a sorry, and each closes at [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]. Supply the hypotheses and ¬CEP follows mechanically. What §6 records is that nobody can currently supply QCE_p or ACM.

The fully proved fragment is the tempered subcategory in TemperedGap.lean — the 2×2 Gram positivity gap and the Deligne temperedness bound 2√p/(p+1) < 1 — with no sorry and no domain-specific premise. It is a short place to read one complete proof.

5. Every open input, named

Nothing in this development is left unfinished in the sense that matters mechanically: no declaration has a sorry body, so no theorem reports sorryAx. What the complexity-free routes still need is instead carried as named hypotheses, each a Prop stated in full and proved nowhere. These are the inputs, and the statement that consumes each one displays it:

DVCentralExtensionAvailable the Dogon–Vigdorovich central extension of Γ_p, constructed B
DVObstructionAvailable flexible HS-stability forces the extension to be non-hyperlinear A/B — the DV obstruction argument
FaithfulTracialGroupModelAvailable every countable group has a faithful tracial C*-model B — needs the reduced group C*-algebra, which the pinned Mathlib does not provide

CepGapStatement the complexity-free C*-native game gap A — the open problem itself; assumed by nothing, used by nothing

Two of these deserve comment.

The vertex stability and the recombination are one input, not two. They were previously two declarations, and splitting them was unsound: the recombination lemma quantified its multiplicative-defect bound with no relation to the accuracy budgets, and in that form the claim is false — take the trivial representation on both vertex copies and the value -1 on an alternating reduced word outside either vertex subgroup, and every hypothesis holds while the conclusion fails. Sharing one modulus between the vertex repair and the recombination is the mathematical content a genuine relative-stability theorem for the amalgam would supply, so that is what the hypothesis now says.

CepGapStatement is a Prop, not a theorem. A proof of it would give ¬CEP without MIP*=RE. Nothing here proves it, nothing assumes it, and no result depends on it; it is recorded because naming the target precisely is worth more than leaving it implicit.

Declarations that no theorem consumed are gone rather than documented: three statements from the IRS dictionary, an iff packaging over an already-proved direction, existence of the hyperfinite II₁ factor, a germ-amenability route, and the classical Serre Trees material. One isolated trace-seminorm computation that had been left open is now proved.

None of the above touches the theorem of §3, which is a mechanical fact rather than a promise: its list of assumptions contains no sorryAx.

One point of fidelity: the amalgam's right edge inclusion is untwisted, so the formal object is the double SL₂(ℤ) ∗_{Γ₀(p)} SL₂(ℤ). The Atkin–Lehner twist identifying it with SL₂(ℤ[1/p]) is needed only to prove QCE_p, not for the conditional reduction.

6. What the complexity-free routes do not establish

These are limitations of specified arguments, not impossibility theorems.

  1. The rank-one core. The data distinguishing edge rigidity sits on a block of dimension Θ(k) inside congruence representations of dimension p^{k-1}(p+1), so its relative mass vanishes; optimal alignment succeeds asymptotically, the torus-phase obstruction washing out at rate √k·p^{-(k-1)/2} (confirmed numerically). The computed obstruction therefore cannot establish QCE_p. The truth of QCE_p is not settled here. Structurally, SL₂(ℤ[1/p]) has no property (T), and nothing rigidifies the amenable congruence tower.

  2. Rigid, same-dimension stability. By the Becker–Lubotzky mechanism an infinite hyperlinear property-(T) group cannot be HS-stable. Within this stability strategy the rigid same-dimension variant is therefore unavailable; the flexible variant remains open, and it is exactly the one whose higher-rank instances are open.

  3. The higher-rank core is ¬CEP-hard. Flexible HS-stability of a 2-Kazhdan higher-rank lattice — Dogon–Vigdorovich's own Problem 1.3 — would already produce an explicit non-hyperlinear group. The sharpest attack tried here, a coupled op-norm/HS Hölder bootstrap against the (T;FD) spectral gap, does not close: the two halves require mutually exclusive regimes, since the op-2 defect is rank-localized and hiding it demands a growing complement, which destroys the Hölder constant the other half needs. This does not exclude another proof.

  4. The monomiality route. The proposed congruence-monomiality argument does not attain the required o(dim) budget on the cuspidal sector.

  5. The non-soficity route targets a different invariant. The cosystolic-expansion programme produces non-sofic groups, and non-soficity alone does not imply non-hyperlinearity — sofic implies hyperlinear, not conversely — so such a group yields no refutation of CEP by this route.

  6. The intrinsic invariants. Cost, ℓ²-Betti numbers, cocycle superrigidity and sofic entropy are orbit-equivalence, ℓ²-spectral or sofic-relative quantities, whereas hyperlinearity is a trace-approximability property. Treeable relations are sofic at every cost and every ℓ²-Betti value. No implication from these invariants to non-hyperlinearity is established here or cited in the generality these routes would need, and the known candidate witnesses are built to satisfy Lück's determinant conjecture.

The value gap MIP*=RE detects is not visible to these invariants. That is the reason for isolating the complexity input as one named premise rather than obscuring it. The implication in §3 is independent of every diagnosis in this section.

7. Spectral truncations

Independent of CEP, and complete: the finite stage of the spectral-truncation programme of Connes and van Suijlekom, machine-checked with no domain-specific premise. Let S be the unilateral shift on ℓ²(ℕ), N the number operator, Q_M = 1_{[0,M]}(N) the cutoff projection, S_M = Q_M S Q_M, and N_M = Q_M N Q_M.

Proved (T_FIB_OS_TRUNCATION_OPERATOR_SYSTEM.lean): Q_M is self-adjoint and idempotent; S_M and S_M* are mutual adjoints in matrix elements; [N_M, ·] annihilates the diagonal modes while [N_M, S_M] = S_M on basis vectors, so ‖S_M‖ > 0 for M ≥ 1; and

S_M* S_M ≠ Q_M      (M ≥ 1)

This is a cutoff-defect identity: in C*(S) one has S*S = 1, so the compression of the product is Q_M, whereas the product of the compressions is diag(1,…,1,0). Compression does not respect products. Note what is not asserted: for A = C*(S) the projection Q_M is finite-rank and the Toeplitz algebra contains the compacts, so the corner Q_M A Q_M is an algebra; the operator-system phenomenon lives in the truncated generating system, not in the corner, and no claim that Q_M C*(S) Q_M fails to be multiplicatively closed is made here.

Proved (T_FIB_OS_FULL_BAND_KERNEL.lean, T_FIB_OS_ALL_COORDINATE_KERNEL.lean): the band-eigen identities [N_M, S_M^p] = p·S_M^p and [N_M, (S_M*)^p] = −p·(S_M*)^p on basis vectors, and the kernel criterion for the simultaneous all-band symbol c₀Q_M + Σ_p (c_p S_M^p + d_p (S_M*)^p): the commutator vanishes on every basis vector exactly when c_p = d_p = 0 for all 1 ≤ p ≤ M. So ker L_M is precisely the cutoff scalars ℂ·Q_M.

Proved (T_FIB_OS_NORM_EQUIV.lean): the finite-dimensional equivalence of norms ‖a'‖_op ≤ C_M · L_M(a') on the off-diagonal complement, whose engine is injectivity of L_M there.

Proved (T_FIB_OS_CQMS_FULL.lean): the finite-stage Rieffel compact-quantum-metric-space structure, assembled as one unconditional theorem fibOS_fullRieffelCQMSFiniteStage whose statement spells out all four requirements — L_M is a *-seminorm (non-negative, absolutely homogeneous, subadditive, star-invariant); the all-coordinate kernel condition above; weak-* metrizability, in Rieffel's total-boundedness form (for every ε > 0 a finite set of centres approximating every Lipschitz-unit-ball element modulo cutoff scalars); and closedness of that unit ball.

8. What can be inspected here, and what re-checking requires

No Lean installation is needed to read the statements: every declaration named above has a page on this site, with its type, its documentation and links to each definition appearing in it. Tactic proofs are not shown.

The certificates quoted in §3 and §7 are reported output of #print axioms. This site does not reproduce them; a fresh kernel re-check requires the Lean sources, which are private and available on request. With those sources, three commands reproduce everything:

lake exe cache get
lake build
lake env lean scripts/AxiomCheck.lean

The third prints the certificates and fails if the main theorem's list ever differs from the four entries in §3 — and likewise for the truncation certificates of §7 — so those lists are invariants rather than claims in prose. grep -rn "^axiom" ZFib returns a single line.
